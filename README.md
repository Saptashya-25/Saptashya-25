I am a Research Associate at the Indian School of Business. I hold a Master’s degree in Economics from Shiv Nadar University, Delhi-NCR and a Bachelor’s degree in Economics from the University of Calcutta, Kolkata.

I am interested in studying consumer choices and firm decisions in digital markets, particularly how consumers allocate their time across competing products and how these choices evolve with technological change. On the firm side, I am interested in understanding how digital platforms compete for consumer attention and adapt their strategies as preferences and patterns of technology use evolve. I use econometric and structural modelling approaches to study consumers and firms in digital markets, with a particular focus on the implications for market outcomes and social welfare.

I trained as an economist and arrived here by a slightly winding route. Before
ISB I spent two years at CAFRAL, the Reserve Bank of India's research centre,
working on climate risk and household finance. My master's thesis studied how
electoral cycles shape the transfer of bureaucrats in India.

The subject matter has moved a good deal but the kind of work hasn't. Across all of
it I've been building large administrative and behavioural datasets and using
causal designs to say something credible about how an outside force - a drought,
an election, a new technology — changes what consumers and firms actually do. 

I work mostly in R and Stata for statistical analysis, use QGIS for spatial data, and Python for scraping
and larger data pipelines.

I'm preparing to apply for PhD programmes and would be glad to hear from anyone working on adjacent questions.
