I'm a Research Associate in the Information Systems area at the Indian School of
Business, where I work on digital platforms and the economics of technological
change. Broadly, I'm interested in how the arrival of a new technology
reorganises an existing ecosystem - what it displaces, what it leaves untouched,
and what that means for competition and for the people using it.

Although most of my work is reduced-form, but I've increasingly been drawn to structural
modelling. I'm currently building a model of how people divide their attention
across digital services and how habits form around them - the kind of question
where quasi-experimental methods tell you what happened, but you need structure
to ask what would happen under a policy or a market that doesn't yet exist.

I trained as an economist and arrived here by a slightly winding route. Before
ISB I spent two years at CAFRAL, the Reserve Bank of India's research centre,
working on climate risk and household finance. My master's thesis studied how
electoral cycles shape the transfer of bureaucrats in India.

The subject matter has moved a good deal. The kind of work hasn't. Across all of
it I've been building large administrative and behavioural datasets and using
causal designs to say something credible about how an outside force - a drought,
an election, a new technology — changes what people and firms actually do.

I work mostly in R and Stata for statistical analysis, use QGIS for spatial data, and Python for scraping
and larger data pipelines.

I'm preparing to apply for PhD programmes and would be glad to hear from anyone working on adjacent questions.
